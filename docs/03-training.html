What is a "step"? The batch arithmetic

The config says per_device_train_batch_size: 2 and gradient_accumulation_steps: 8. The GPU processes 2 samples at a time, but gradients are accumulated across 8 rounds before the weights move once — an effective batch of 2 × 8 = 16 samples per step. Accumulation is another memory trick: the behavior of a 16-sample batch at the VRAM cost of a 2-sample batch.

720 training samples ÷ 16 per step = 45 steps per epoch. Three epochs — three full passes over the same data — give the run's 135 total steps. (Why 720 and not 800? Chapter 1's gotcha: val_size: 0.1 carved the validation set out of the train file.)

The loss curve

Loss is the model's error score on the batch it just saw — specifically cross-entropy: how surprised the model is by the correct next token (lower means the right answer was assigned higher probability). Training exists to push it down. Watch what happens at each epoch boundary, where the model starts re-reading data it has already seen:

Three phases are visible:

  • Steps 1–40, the fast drop (2.13 → 1.19): the model quickly picks up the surface format — Alpaca structure, the yes/no-then-explanation shape, the ever-present disclaimer. Cheap, legitimate gains.
  • Steps 40–90, the plateau (~1.2 → 1.0): epoch 2. Genuine learning is slowing; the model has extracted most of what 720 samples generalize to.
  • Steps 90–135, the suspicious drop (1.01 → 0.71): epoch 3 begins and loss falls off a cliff — on the third reading of identical data. That's not new understanding; that's recognition. Chapter 4 confirms it with the held-out numbers.

The learning-rate schedule

The learning rate is the size of each weight update. The config asks for warmup_ratio: 0.1 and lr_scheduler_type: cosine, and the log shows exactly that shape:

  • Warmup (first 10% of steps): starting gently protects the freshly initialized adapter from violent early updates while gradients are still noisy.
  • Cosine decay: updates shrink smoothly toward zero, letting the model settle into a minimum instead of bouncing around it. By step 130 the learning rate is about 1/165 of its peak — training ends by fading out, not by slamming a door.

What the log actually looks like

No magic — each line of trainer_log.jsonl is plain JSON. This is the run's first line, and it maps 1:1 onto the charts above:

{"current_steps": 10, "total_steps": 135, "loss": 2.1294,
 "lr": 3.2142857142857144e-05, "epoch": 0.222, "percentage": 7.41,
 "elapsed_time": "0:00:44", "remaining_time": "0:09:16"}

The same numbers also stream to TensorBoard (./scripts/tensorboard.sh, then http://localhost:6006) for live monitoring, and save_steps: 100 wrote full checkpoints at step 100 and at the end — which is why the repo has both checkpoint-100/ and checkpoint-135/ to compare.

The run's blind spot: eval_steps: 100 meant held-out evaluation happened only twice in the whole run — at step 100 and at the finish. The train curve above looks like a success story; the two eval measurements tell a different one. That story is next.
